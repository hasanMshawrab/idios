1Incidents - problems, not incidents (home)

The home screen answers "what needs attention": attention is open, or closed within attention_window and never acknowledged or dismissed, so a row that closed an hour ago and nobody has looked at is still in front of the person, and once the window passes it leaves the default view on its own. The unit of the list is the problem. Rows group by workload by default, and every group header has one shape: a section header with a chevron, the same inset rule as its rows and a faint tint, carrying kind and name, a plain-language summary ("every 2m, 62 of 63 runs failed, since 07:32" for a CronJob; "3 of 3 pods looping, tag 1.36" for a Deployment), the worst open category and one state badge with the open count. A cluster or namespace header appears only while more than one is in scope and is drawn as the same header shape. Inside a CronJob's or Job's group the unit is the run: the job_failed row and the pod rows of one Job (job_uid) fold into one run row naming the run's suffix, its pod count, the categories found among them, the exit code, the reason and the open count; the newest five runs show and a "+ N more runs" line opens Workloads on that CronJob's Runs. Replica fan-out keeps its rollup for the other kinds: the rows of one workload that share a container and a category across two or more pods become one row naming the count of pods, the reasons and exit codes found among them, expanding to the pod rows; last_reason is not in the key because a crash loop alternates its reasons. A bare pod never rolls up; a job-subject incident folds into its run. Expansion state of groups, runs and rollups persists per group id across the window's life and across a grouping change. Every count is a count of incidents; nothing sums restarts. A row is one line with real columns: state dot, category chip, subject (kind and name, a pod's suffix after the workload prefix), container ("job" for a job-subject row), reason with exit code, restarts ("-" for a job-subject row), age, state badge; the last three have fixed widths and the age never wraps, because the word open or closed lives in the state badge. The second line (namespace, pod, tag, deletion) is the row's hover and a Comfortable density in the View menu. Closed rows dim. State owns hue and categories are nouns: a red dot is an open incident and nothing else; a category is a neutral chip with a glyph. manual is labelled Resolved everywhere. The sidebar is a source list: Screens, then View (Attention, Open, Acknowledged, a Closed disclosure holding Recovered, Pod deleted, Job finished, Resolved, Dismissed), then Category; every row keeps its count, zero included, categories with no incident fold into one dimmed row, an active row takes the selection accent and never its state colour. View and Category show only on this screen; Screens stays on every screen, a pushed pod page included. The "?" beside View opens the legend (1c); every state and category name carries a one-sentence tooltip; an empty view teaches the state's definition, the key that produces it, and a way back to Attention. Cluster is a scope, not a grouping: the titlebar menu decides which rows exist and every count recomputes for it. Group by is one of five modes: workload, namespace, category, cluster and time. Search jumps and filter narrows: Cmd-K opens the palette (1b) over any screen and "/" focuses the filter field, which narrows the served rows. The keyboard reaches all of it: the arrow keys walk the rows, Space expands, Return opens the selected row or run, a, d, r and n acknowledge, dismiss, resolve and annotate the selected row or every incident under a group, run or rollup (d and r ask first, naming the count and the workload), Command-Delete deletes one incident behind a confirmation, and the Go menu holds Cmd-1 Incidents, Cmd-2 Workloads, Cmd-3 Status, Cmd-K Search, Cmd-[ Back and Cmd-] Forward. Every example name is invented.

2Pod page - one screen keyed by pod: the pod and its containers, the selection, the rail

The pod has one page, keyed by its uid, and it is where a person reads what is going on inside that pod: the incident detail, the pod screen and the timeline screen are this one screen. Every route still opens it: incident/<id> fetches the incident and opens its pod with that container selected and that incident lit on Overview, timeline/<id> does the same on Timeline, pod/<uid> opens the Pod card on Events, and pod/<uid>/<tab> maps the old tab names onto the Pod card (containers, incidents and events open Events, conditions opens Conditions, logs opens Files, podjson opens pod.json). The back chevron returns to wherever the page was opened from. The pod-and-containers column is the leading pane of the pushed screen at the sidebar's width, so the window keeps its rhythm: the sidebar keeps its Screens section while the page is up and drops View and Category, because those filter the list and nothing else; Cmd-1/2/3 still switch screens. The column is the Pod as a card, then the containers as cards indented under it with a connector, each carrying its state line and one badge per incident category with its occurrences; the selected card fills with the accent. GetPod is authoritative for the page: the pod row, the containers, the latest conditions, the incident list that puts the badges on the cards, the artifacts grouped by container, and the siblings. Selecting a container (2a) shows, above its tabs, the middle pane's own header: the lit incident's category and state tags, its sentence, its actions and its note. Overview is the kubelet facts from the container row, the container's files and its events; Timeline is the lit incident's window; Logs are the captured files. The container's events are the pod's events whose field_path names it plus every pod-level event (Scheduled, Killing at pod scope, Evicted, Preempted) marked as the pod's, because the event that explains a death is usually the pod's. When the container has incidents, a segmented control above the pane header has one segment per incident in the fold order (open before closed, worst category first, ties to the newest); the lit segment's GetIncident supplies the header, the note and the Timeline tab, and switching segments changes those and nothing else. The previous response is kept until the next one arrives, so a switch never blanks the header. Selecting the Pod card (2b) shows the pod's tabs: Events (every container, a container column, time order), Conditions (latest per type and the history), Files (every container's files together and pod.json), pod.json, and Related incidents only when the pod belongs to a Job: the job_failed row and the retries that ran in other pods, the one set of related rows the page cannot show anywhere else. Nothing else is "related": this pod's incidents are the left column, this ReplicaSet's pods are Siblings in the rail, the same problem across pods is the list's rollup. The page header is the pod's and never moves: the state tag (RUNNING, READY, RUNNING, NOT READY, DELETED), the counts line (containers, incidents open, node), the title Pod <name>, and the identity line: cluster / namespace / the owner chain as it exists (Deployment + ReplicaSet, or StatefulSet, or CronJob + Job, or nothing for a bare pod), stopping before the pod because the title is the pod. The incident's own sentence and actions live in the middle pane's header, never up here. The rail is the pod's rail, top to bottom: the owner chain in the dotted form with the container as a fourth dot when one is selected; context; the times of what is selected (the lit incident's opened, last seen, open for, reopened, will close; or the pod's created, started, first and last seen, deletion requested, deleted); siblings. Siblings are capped, ordered and carry their category: pods with an open incident first (worst category first), then live pods, then deleted ones, newest created first inside each group, five shown with a category badge where there is one and a state where there is none, and a "+ N more" line that opens Workloads > that workload > Pods, whose list is the Deployment's pods across ReplicaSets and may be longer; the label says which set it counts. The incident stream is one subscription for the life of the page, filtered on this pod's incidents; an update to any of them re-reads GetPod so the badges stay true. Opening incident/<id> is GetIncident, then GetPod and PodEvents; opening pod/<uid> is GetPod and PodEvents; a segment switch is one GetIncident; a live update is one GetPod. The middle pane leads with a verdict block built from fields the page already receives: what happened, how many times since when and last when, the memory limit from mem_limit_bytes, what was and was not captured from capture_gap, and whether the image digest changed since the incident opened, by comparing the image_id recorded at open with the container's. Acknowledge is the one primary button and shows its key, Note stands beside it, and the rest of the verbs are in an Actions menu. The segmented control appears only when a container has two or more incidents. Captured logs are a scrubber over one file body, restart N of M with previous and next, and the tab's "Logs N" counts the files that exist. Method text lives on the card titles' hovers, never as visible text. The identity line is a breadcrumb whose segments open Workloads at that node, and Back has a Forward beside it (Cmd-]). The rail keeps the owner chain, Node, QoS class, Image id, Container id, the times and the siblings, and repeats neither cluster, namespace nor image. Repeating cycles in the Timeline fold into one entry ("x31, every ~4 min, first 07:32, last 09:52") behind a disclosure; lifecycle, rollout and cut entries never fold, and each entry carries one time with the exact pair on hover. The header tag is debounced and reads RUNNING, LOOPING when readiness flipped more than three times in the window. Plurals agree. The events table never wraps a timestamp: the time column widens and the message wraps. Every example name is invented.

3Pod page - the container pane on its Timeline tab

The container pane on its Timeline tab, the same page as 2a with one tab moved. 5Workloads - which service fails most, which version broke, which runs failed

Grouped by incidents.workload_kind, workload_name, the one identity that survives rollouts, except the pods no controller owns, which are one row per pod name. A namespace groups its workloads by kind in a fixed order, long-lived first and bare pods last, under plural captions with counts ("DEPLOYMENTS 1", "CRONJOBS 2", "BARE PODS 5"); the pane header names what the trailing number is ("open incidents") and holds Collapse all and Expand all; a filter narrows the tree and never clears collapse state. Kinds have no colour. Pods no controller owns are one row per name carrying "N pods" when a deleted pod of the same name is kept; the row opens the live pod, or a chooser when both are kept. Every row has a context menu: Show incidents, Show pods, Copy name, Acknowledge all open. The detail is tabs whose titles count what they hold. A CronJob opens on Runs with a run strip above the table: one cell per run in the window, oldest first, coloured by outcome (failed red, Complete green), dashed where a sweep removed the record, the newest outlined; hover gives condition, duration and exit code, click opens the run's pod, drag acknowledges the incidents under the selection; a strip up to 200 runs, an hour-by-minute matrix beyond. The Runs table defaults to Failed when failed_total > 0, names runs by the suffix after <cronjob>-, links each run to its pod and its incident, folds consecutive runs with the same reason, and colours a run's Condition badge by its incident's state: red while open, grey once closed. Success and failure come from condition_type and condition_reason, never from counters. The Pods tab defaults to Live. The Overview has the 3d / 24h / 6h window and a restarts chart with a y-axis, hover values and true hourly bars, an hour containing gap_reconstructed rows drawn dashed. The View and Category sections of the sidebar are not on this screen, since they filter nothing here. Only workloads seen inside the retention window appear; this is a three-day view, not an SLO. Every example name is invented.

8App icon and menu bar glyph - the mark, its reductions, and where each one is used

The mark is the name's own initial built out of cluster material: the stem is a stack of kept records, the tittle is the lamp that means something is being recorded. It is four shapes on a graphite slab, and the slab is graphite rather than blue on purpose - blue is already spent on the selection accent, and an icon in accent blue competes with every selected row on the screens above. Red appears exactly once, as the thing that broke. The red tittle is fixed artwork, not a status light: the Dock badge is what counts open incidents, and an icon that recoloured with cluster health would fight it. Only the menu bar glyph tints, because it is a template image - macOS keeps its alpha and throws away every colour, which is why the glyph carries no second colour to lose. The 32pt and 16pt icons are not downscales: the record stack drops from three to two to one so the silhouette stays two shapes at every size. All of it is generated by hack/macos/icon.py into macos/idios/Assets.xcassets, so this page and the shipped asset are the same geometry.

CComponents

Shared vocabulary used by every screen above. Each badge text is a value idios stores verbatim; the colour is the only thing the UI adds, and one rule governs it: state owns hue. Red is open and unacknowledged, amber is acknowledged (and a degraded container state), grey is closed by any reason including job_finished, green is live and healthy only (running and ready, a synced cluster, a Complete run) and never a closed failure, the accent is selection only. Categories are nouns: a neutral outlined chip with a small glyph, no hue. Kinds have no colour. The rule applies in one pass to list badges, rail dots, tree dots, timeline dots, the run strip and the pod header tag, and the application keeps its colours in one file. One rule governs every text field: no value is ever silently cut. Rows grow, prose wraps, logs scroll, and the single permitted elision (the middle of a long identifier) always shows the full value on hover with a copy control. C4 shows the four treatments.
